Getting around

Good to know

  • Room service runs 24 hours and is included — with your Excellence Club stay you get the upgraded Club menu. The resort only publishes the room service menu inside the Excellence Collection app, so browse it there; there's no public copy to put on this page.
  • The bars pour a full premium list but publish no itemized drink menus — the Martini Bar's "tini" list, Revive's smoothies, and every other bar's list live at the bar itself. Hours are on each bar's pin.
  • Dinner dress codes: "Casual Elegance" (Magna, Chez Isabelle, Spice) means long pants and a collared shirt — no shorts or flip-flops. "Regular Casual" allows Bermudas and a polo.
  • Only one reservation: the teppanyaki tables at Spice. Everything else is walk-in.
  • Book the spa hydrotherapy circuit early — Friday or Saturday of your stay — and ask about the temazcal session while you're there.
  • Two temporary moves: Chez Isabelle's menu is currently served at Flavor Market, and Agave's at Oregano, while those rooms are refreshed. Barcelona and Toscana have closed for good.
  • Fresh catch on the beach: the closest thing to fishermen cooking their catch near the resort is down in Puerto Morelos town (pins 38–44) — El Pesquero and Pelícanos by the pier serve the local boats' fish; ask what's fresh that day. There's no catch-of-the-day stand on the resort's own beach.

Hidden gems travelers keep recommending

Beyond the town pins above, these are the names that come up again and again in traveler forums and local guides — including r/snorkeling threads and Puerto Morelos residents. The cenotes sit inland on the Ruta de los Cenotes: a taxi or tour drive of about 20–30 minutes, not a walk.

  • Reef snorkeling from the town pier — r/snorkeling regulars send first-timers here instead of the hotel beach: the reef sits just offshore and small local boats at the pier run short trips (forum posts put them around $25 a person). Go early, before the wind picks up. How the advice breaks down →
  • Cenote La Noria — the local-feel pick on the Ruta: a cavern cenote with a big opening overhead, a floating platform, and picnic tables — travelers contrast it with the big tour-bus stops. Ruta de los Cenotes guide →
  • Cenote Zapote — the deep one, famous among divers for the "Hells Bells" mineral formations; the park around it adds ziplines and ATV trails if swimming isn't enough. Cenote route overview →
  • Cenote Siete Bocas — seven openings in the cave roof; you swim from one shaft of light to the next under the stalactites. Cenote route overview →
  • Cenote Verde Lucero — open-air, emerald-green, and usually the quietest of the four, at about km 17 on the same road. Ruta de los Cenotes guide →
